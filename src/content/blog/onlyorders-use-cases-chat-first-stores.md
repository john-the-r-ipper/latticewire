The best way to understand a chat-commerce tool is not to read about features — it's to watch a single conversation turn into a confirmed order. ItOrders publishes a live demo precisely for this, and it's a miniature masterclass in how Messenger buying should feel.

> **Customer:** "Is the blue kurti available in M?"  
> **AI:** "Yes — 3 left in M. Want me to reserve one?"  
> **Customer:** "Yes please, cash on delivery."  
> **AI:** "Done. Order #OO-2481 is confirmed ✓"

Four exchanges, and an order exists. This post walks through the use cases that pattern represents, and where they show up in real chat-first stores.

## The Messenger storefront

In markets where Facebook is the social hub, Messenger isn't a "channel" — it's the storefront. Shoppers message a page the way they'd walk into a shop. The first job of any chat automation is to greet them naturally: the OnlyOrders demo opens with a warm salutation and category suggestions (kurti, three-piece, saree, panjabi), asking what the customer is looking for. That single touch does the work of a physical store's "welcome, can I help you?"

## Stock and size questions

The most common message a seller gets is a variant of *"do you have this in size M?"* This is where AI replies earn their keep — answering price, size, and stock questions in seconds, in the shop's own tone, rather than making the buyer wait for a human to be free. The demo shows exactly this: inventory check ("3 left in M") plus a proactive upsell-like nudge ("want me to reserve one?"). Reserving, rather than just confirming stock, is the behavior that converts a question into a potential sale.

## Photo match and catalog checks

Beyond text, buyers increasingly shop by image — sending a photo of a product and asking "do you have this?" The OnlyOrders feature set includes photo match and catalog checks in the conversation flow. For a seller with a large catalog, this collapses what would otherwise be a slow, error-prone lookup into an instant answer from the same thread.

## Checkout inside the chat

This is the step that separates chat ordering from chat *inquiry*. Rather than routing the customer to an external cart, the buyer confirms the cart, address, and payment without leaving Messenger. Cash on delivery remains the default in many markets — the demo's customer chooses it — and the platform's Pro tier adds payment verification for mobile wallets like bKash and Nagad, which are the backbone of online payment in Bangladesh. The transaction keeps its high-trust shape because it never leaves the context where the buyer felt comfortable.

## Courier handoff

An order isn't done when it's confirmed; it's done when it's delivered. OnlyOrders closes the loop by letting the seller book the courier in one tap, with tracking updates pushed back into the buyer's chat. The customer gets delivery status in the same thread where they ordered — no separate tracking portal, no "where's my order?" ping-pong.

## Where this fits for a small seller

None of these use cases require a big team. In fact, each one is disproportionately valuable precisely when there *isn't* slack in the operation:

- A seller who can't answer at 11 p.m. benefits from a 24/7 first reply.
- A seller juggling Messenger, WhatsApp, and Instagram benefits from one inbox instead of three.
- A seller who loses orders to unreplied messages benefits from anything that reliably captures the order while it's fresh.

## The honest framing

As always, we'll be plain about scope. AI replies are receptionists, not owners — they hand off when the question is specific, sensitive, or emotional. But for the routine 80% of chat commerce — stock checks, catalog lookups, cart confirmation, courier updates — the automation is where the message-to-order distance gets compressed. That's the entire value proposition, and it's measurable.
